# Find an existing patient

Use this guide when a patient who has been to your facility before comes back. Always look for their record first. Registering them again would create a second record and split their history in two.

**Who this is for:** front desk and records staff, and anyone else who needs to open a patient's record.

**How long it takes:** under a minute.

## Two ways to find a patient

| Method | Best when | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Quick search** | You know the patient's name or ID number. This is the fastest way and the one you'll use most. | The **Search** box at the top of every screen |
| **Patient list** | You need to browse, sort, or narrow the list down, for example everyone with a certain surname | **Favorites → Patient** in the left menu |

## Before you start

You need:

- An Afiax account. You're signed in when you can see the **Search** box at the top of the screen.
- One of these from the patient:
  - Their name. Ask for the name on their ID, because that's how they were registered.
  - Their ID number, for example their National ID or passport number.

:::tip
The ID number is the most reliable way to search. Names can be spelled differently from visit to visit, but the ID number is always the same.
:::

## Method 1: Quick search

1. Click the **Search** box at the top of the screen.
2. Type one of these:
   - **The ID number,** in full and exactly as it appears on the document, for example `12345678`. Part of a number won't find anything.
   - **The name:** the patient's first name or surname, or the first few letters of it, for example `Kamau` or `Kam`.
3. Wait a moment. A list of matching patients drops down under the box. Each one shows the patient's name and, underneath it in grey, their date of birth.
4. **Check the date of birth with the patient** before you choose. Two people can share a name, but rarely a name and a date of birth.
5. Click the right patient.

{/* SCREENSHOT: Search box with a name typed and the drop-down list of matches */}

**What you should see:** the patient's record opens. Their name and details are shown at the top of the screen.

:::note
The drop-down shows at most 5 patients that match the name, plus up to 5 that match the ID number. If you can't see the patient you want, type more of the name, or search by ID number instead.
:::

## Method 2: The patient list

### Open the list

1. In the left menu, under **Favorites**, click **Patient**.

You'll see a table of patients registered at your facility. It shows each patient's name, date of birth and gender, and when their record was last updated.

{/* SCREENSHOT: Patient list */}

### Narrow the list down by name

1. Click the **Name** column heading. A menu opens.
2. Click **Contains...**.
3. In the box that appears, type all or part of the name, for example `Wanjiru`.
4. Click **OK**.

The table now shows only patients whose name contains what you typed. Under the **Name** heading, a short line of text shows the filter you set, for example *contains Wanjiru*.

{/* SCREENSHOT: Name column menu open, with Contains... highlighted */}

### Narrow the list down by date of birth

1. Click the **Birth Date** column heading.
2. Click **Equals...**.
3. Choose the date and click **OK**.

You can use the name and date-of-birth filters together. For example, filter by name `Otieno` and date of birth 4 March 1988 to find one patient among many with the same surname.

### Sort the list

Click a column heading and choose **Sort A to Z** or **Sort Z to A** for names, or **Sort Oldest to Newest** or **Sort Newest to Oldest** for dates.

### Remove your filters

Click the column heading you filtered on and choose **Clear filters**. The full list comes back.

### Open a patient

Click anywhere on the patient's row. Their record opens.

:::tip
To open a patient in a new browser tab, click their row with the middle mouse button, or the scroll wheel. This keeps your filtered list open in the first tab.
:::

## If something goes wrong

### Nothing appears when you search

1. **If you searched by ID number,** check you typed the whole number, with no spaces, and that it's the ID the patient used when they registered. A patient registered with their passport won't be found by their National ID.
2. **If you searched by name,** try the surname on its own, or fewer letters. Check the spelling with the patient, or ask them to show you their ID.
3. **If you still can't find them,** they may never have been registered at your facility. Go to [Register a new patient](./register-a-patient.md). Registration checks for an existing record before it creates a new one, so you won't create a duplicate by mistake.

### Several patients look like the right one

Compare the date of birth with the patient's ID. If two records seem to be the same person, open them both and check the ID number on each. If they really are the same patient, don't use either one yet: tell your facility administrator, who can get the duplicate records sorted out.

### The table looks empty or shows the wrong patients

You may still have a filter on. Look under the column headings for a line of filter text. Click that column's heading and choose **Clear filters**. You can also click **Patient** in the left menu again, which opens the list with no filters.

## Good to know

- **Searching doesn't change anything.** You can search as often as you like. Nothing in the patient's record changes until you open it and edit it.
- **Filters last until you leave the list.** When you open **Patient** from the left menu again, the list starts with no filters.
- **Only patients registered at your facility are shown.** Patients registered somewhere else don't appear until they've been registered at your facility.
